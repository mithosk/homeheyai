## Physical Characteristics

* **The Trunk:** An extraordinary fusion of the upper lip and nose containing over 40,000 muscles. It is used for breathing, smelling, drinking, picking up objects, trumpeting, and social touching.
* **Tusks:** Elongated incisor teeth that grow throughout an elephant's life. They are used for digging, lifting objects, stripping bark from trees, and defense.
* **Large Ears:** Act as a natural cooling system. By flapping their large ears, elephants cool the blood flowing through blood vessels near the skin's surface.
* **Size & Weight:** 
  * The largest existing land animal.
  * Weight ranges from 2,700 kg (6,000 lbs) for Asian females up to 6,000 kg (13,000+ lbs) for African male savannas.

## Behavior & Intelligence

Elephants are renowned for their complex emotional lives, social bonds, and high intelligence:

* **Matriarchal Society:** Herds are led by an older, experienced female called the **matriarch**. Herds consist of related females and their calves. Adult males (bulls) are mostly solitary or form small male groups.
* **Communication:** Utilize low-frequency sounds called **infrasound** that can travel through the ground for miles, allowing long-distance communication undetectable to human ears.
* **Empathy & Memory:** Elephants display grief over deceased herd members, assist injured individuals, and possess remarkable spatial memory, remembering migration routes and water sources over decades.

## Diet & Ecological Role

Elephants are herbivorous megaherbivores that require vast amounts of food daily.

* **Diet:** Grasses, leaves, bark, fruits, and roots. An adult can consume up to **150–200 kg (330–440 lbs)** of vegetation per day.
* **Keystone Species:** 
  * **Landscape Architects:** They knock down trees, creating open clearings for other species.
  * **Water Diggers:** Dig holes in dry riverbeds to access underground water, which benefits other wildlife.
  * **Seed Dispersers:** Transport and deposit plant seeds across vast distances through their dung.

## Species & Geographic Range

There are three living species of elephants recognized today:

1. **African Savanna Elephant (*Loxodonta africana*):**
   * The largest of all species; distinguished by larger ears shaped like the continent of Africa and tusks in both sexes.
2. **African Forest Elephant (*Loxodonta cyclotis*):**
   * Smaller, denser-bodied elephants inhabiting the tropical rainforests of Central and West Africa.
3. **Asian Elephant (*Elephas maximus*):**
   * Smaller ears with a curved back; only some males have visible tusks. Subspecies include the Indian, Sri Lankan, and Sumatran elephants.

## Threats & Conservation Status

* **Poaching for Ivory:** Illegal trade in elephant tusks remains a severe global crisis despite international bans.
* **Habitat Loss & Fragmentation:** Expanding human settlements, roads, and agriculture destroy natural migration corridors.
* **Human-Elephant Conflict:** Crop-raiding by hungry herds leads to retaliatory killings by farmers.

> **Conservation Focus:** Establishing protected corridors, community-based anti-poaching units, and using creative deterrents like beehive fences to protect crops without harming elephants.

## Cultural Impact

* **Symbol of Wisdom:** Revered across many Asian cultures, representing strength, wisdom, and good fortune (e.g., the Hindu deity *Ganesha*).
* **Historical Roles:** Used historically in transportation, forestry, and ancient military warfare (notably by Hannibal crossing the Alps).
