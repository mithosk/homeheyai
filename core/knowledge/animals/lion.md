## Physical Characteristics

* **Sexual Dimorphism:** Male lions are easily recognized by their prominent, dark manes, which protect their necks during fights and signal health and dominance to potential mates. Females (lionesses) lack manes, making them leaner and more agile.
* **Size & Weight:** 
  * **Males:** 150–250 kg (330–550 lbs)
  * **Females:** 110–180 kg (240–400 lbs)
* **Bite Force & Roar:** A lion's roar can reach up to **114 decibels** and can be heard from up to 8 kilometers (5 miles) away.

---

## Social Structure & Behavior

Unlike almost all other wild cats, lions are deeply social animals:

### The Pride
* **Composition:** A typical pride consists of around 15 lions, including several related females, their offspring, and a small coalition of dominant males (usually 1–4).
* **Role of Females:** Lionesses are the primary hunters and protectors of the pride's territory against intruding females. They often give birth in synchronization and nurse each other's cubs.
* **Role of Males:** Resident males defend the pride's females and territory from rival males and external predators like hyenas.

---

## Hunting & Diet

Lions are apex hypercarnivores that primarily hunt medium-to-large ungulates.

* **Primary Prey:** Wildebeests, zebras, buffaloes, impalas, and warthogs.
* **Hunting Strategy:** Lionesses utilize cooperative pack tactics. They stalk prey under the cover of night or tall grass, surrounding the target before launching a explosive short-burst sprint.
* **Success Rate:** While individual hunts have a low success rate (~15%), cooperative group hunts increase success significantly (~30% or higher).

## Subspecies & Distribution

1. **African Lion (*Panthera leo leo* / *Panthera leo melanochaita*):** Inhabits sub-Saharan Africa, with major populations in East and Southern Africa.
2. **Asiatic Lion (*Panthera leo persica*):** A critically small, isolated population surviving strictly within the **Gir National Park** in Gujarat, India.

---

## Threats & Conservation Status

Lions face significant threats across their native ranges:

* **Habitat Loss & Fragmentation:** Human expansion and agriculture reduce the available land for prides and prey.
* **Human-Wildlife Conflict:** Farmers often retaliate against lions to protect domestic livestock.
* **Poaching:** Targeted for trophy hunting, traditional medicine, and illegal wildlife trade.

> **Conservation Effort:** Initiatives such as protected wildlife corridors, community-based livestock guarding, and ecotourism help support local lion conservation.
