## Physical Characteristics

* **Largest Big Cat:** The tiger is the largest species among all wild felids.
* **Unique Stripe Patterns:** A tiger's stripes are like human fingerprints—no two individuals have the exact same pattern. The skin beneath the fur is also striped.
* **Size & Weight:**
  * **Males:** 90–300 kg (200–660 lbs), depending on the subspecies.
  * **Females:** 65–167 kg (140–370 lbs).
* **Night Vision:** Tigers have exceptional night vision, up to six times better than that of humans.
* **Canine Teeth:** Possess long, sharp canine teeth (up to 7.5–10 cm / 3–4 inches) designed for grabbing and incapacitating prey.

## Behavior & Social Structure

Unlike social lions, tigers are predominantly **solitary and territorial** creatures:

* **Territoriality:** Males maintain large home territories that overlap with those of several females. They mark boundaries using urine, scent gland secretions, and claw scratches on trees.
* **Affinity for Water:** Unlike most domestic cats and many big cats, tigers are strong swimmers and frequently bathe in rivers, lakes, and ponds to cool off during hot days.
* **Vocalizations:** Communicate via roaring, chuffing (a friendly, non-threatening greeting), growling, and snarling.

## Diet & Hunting Tactics

Tigers are obligate carnivores and formidable apex predators.

* **Primary Prey:** Large ungulates, including deer (sambar, chital), wild boar, gaur, and water buffalo.
* **Ambush Strategy:** Rely on camouflage and stealth to creep close to prey before launching a sudden, powerful strike from behind or the side.
* **Kill Technique:** Deliver a lethal bite to the throat to suffocate larger prey, or bite the back of the neck to break the spinal cord of smaller animals.

## Subspecies & Geographic Range

Taxonomists generally recognize two major continental groupings/subspecies today:

1. **Continental Tiger (*Panthera tigris tigris*):**
   * **Bengal Tiger:** Found primarily in India, Nepal, Bhutan, and Bangladesh.
   * **Amur (Siberian) Tiger:** Inhabits the cold, snow-covered forests of Eastern Russia and Northeast China.
   * **Indochinese & Malayan Tigers:** Inhabit mainland Southeast Asia and the Malay Peninsula.
   * **Caspian Tiger:** *Extinct (mid-20th century).*
2. **Sunda Island Tiger (*Panthera tigris sondaica*):**
   * **Sumatran Tiger:** Critically endangered; confined strictly to the island of Sumatra, Indonesia.
   * **Javan & Bali Tigers:** *Extinct (20th century).*

## Threats & Conservation Status

Tigers are currently listed as **Endangered** on the IUCN Red List. Over the past century, global wild tiger populations dropped by over 95%, though intense conservation efforts have recently stabilized some populations.

### Key Threats:
* **Poaching & Illegal Wildlife Trade:** Targeted for skins, bones, and body parts used in illegal trade and traditional medicine.
* **Habitat Loss & Fragmentation:** Forest clearing for agriculture, infrastructure, and commercial logging isolates populations.
* **Human-Wildlife Conflict:** Retaliatory killings by local communities protecting domestic livestock.

> **Conservation Success:** Countries like **India, Nepal, and Russia** have seen population increases due to strict anti-poaching patrols, protected reserves (e.g., *Project Tiger* in India), and habitat restoration initiatives.

## Cultural Significance

* **National Animal:** The tiger is the national animal of India, Bangladesh, Malaysia, and South Korea.
* **Symbolism:** Represents courage, power, majesty, and strength across Asian mythology and folklore.
* **Zodiac:** One of the 12 animals in the Chinese zodiac cycle, symbolizing bravery and authority.
